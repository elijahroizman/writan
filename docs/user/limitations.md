# What it doesn't do yet

- **Word.** Tracked changes are accepted or rejected all at once, not one by one. A comment covers text within one paragraph, and adding or removing comments isn't part of undo. When printing, table borders are left out. Block content controls show their saved content, but you can't edit it or update a table of contents. Office Math equations show as one line of text, the way Word's linear format writes them, rather than laid out as built-up math.
- **PowerPoint.** Videos are added as their thumbnail with a link, which is how a link to an online video travels between apps. In Writan's slideshow they play on the slide. In PowerPoint or Keynote, clicking one opens the video in the browser. The slideshow plays slide transitions and object animations a click at a time. Appear, Fade, Fly, Wipe, Split, Zoom and Spin look like PowerPoint's, other entrances and exits fade, other emphasis effects pulse, motion paths don't move, and animations started by clicking a shape don't play. Common 2D charts show a preview drawn from the values saved in the chart, which you can't edit and whose formatting is approximate. A chart of a type Writan doesn't draw, or with its own axis scale, can show a placeholder. Saving keeps the chart's original parts.
- **SmartArt in Word and PowerPoint.** Writan draws a diagram from the picture of it Office saves with the file, and it can't be edited. A diagram saved without that picture, as some other apps save them, shows its text in a plain row of boxes instead of its real layout. Saving keeps its original parts.
- **Excel.** Pictures, conditional formatting and data validation show but can't be edited, and saving keeps them as they were. Inserting or deleting rows and columns moves them with their cells. Radar, stock, bubble and surface charts show as a placeholder and are kept as they are. A chart you change is saved with Excel's default styling. Writan works out about 140 functions, including SUMIFS, XLOOKUP, IFS and the statistics and date functions. A formula that uses another function shows the result Excel saved for it until Writan recalculates the workbook, after an edit for example, and then it shows `#NAME?` until Excel recalculates it. Formulas can use existing Excel tables, such as `Sales[Amount]`, but Writan can't make or rename a table. A CSV file holds values only, so charts and formatting made in Writan are lost when it's closed.
- **PDF.** Text, checkbox, radio and list fields can be filled, and a signature field takes a picture of your signature. Buttons and form scripts do nothing, and Writan can't add a certificate signature. Saving a PDF you haven't changed keeps its bytes and its certificate signatures, but editing a signed PDF can make its signature invalid. A form made only with Adobe's XFA shows its placeholder page. Chinese, Japanese or Korean text in a font the PDF doesn't include, as in some older PDFs, might not show.
- **Editing a PDF as a Word document.** Writan rebuilds the PDF, so the Word copy isn't exact. Shapes, boxes and backgrounds other than tables and charts are dropped, and text turned on its side is left out. A table cell that spans columns or wraps over several lines can come apart, and a chart whose labels line up like a table's can come through as its labels only. A font the computer doesn't have is replaced, so pages can run longer than the PDF's.
- **PDFs Writan exports.** In a right-to-left paragraph with English words separated by spaces, Writan's own search and copy can put those English words in reverse order. Other PDF readers read them in the right order.
- **Text in scanned PDFs and pictures.** Languages other than English download once before Writan can read them. Text read from a scanned PDF can be found and selected in Writan, but it isn't saved into the file, and Writan reads it again each time the PDF opens. Faint, low-resolution scans give worse results. When a scan becomes a Word document, blank pages are left out, and Hebrew numbered lists show decimal numbers in Writan, though Word shows them with Hebrew letters. A WebP picture opens for viewing, OCR and searchable PDF export. To draw on it or crop it, Writan opens a JPEG copy.
- **LaTeX.** It needs TeX on the computer. A new, unsaved document can't use other files. Clicking in the PDF doesn't jump to the source, since Writan has no SyncTeX yet. Installing and removing TeX on Windows are still being tested.
- **Spelling.** Excel workbooks, PDF form fields, LaTeX source and text in grouped shapes on a slide aren't spell checked. On Windows, Word and PowerPoint use the installed Windows spell checker for your language. A regional variant such as Australian English needs that language's Basic typing feature installed in Windows Settings, and otherwise falls back to another installed English dictionary. On Linux, Markdown and text files aren't spell checked, and Word and PowerPoint need a Hunspell dictionary, such as `hunspell-en-us`.
- **Sharing.** It works in Word documents, presentations and workbooks, and only while the person sharing has the tab open. A code for sharing over the internet lets people join for ten minutes. When Writan can't reach the internet, sharing works only between computers on the same network. Then the code works for as long as you share, and anyone who types it joins without asking you. A firewall on the sharing computer has to let Writan accept connections, which macOS and Windows ask about the first time. What's sent isn't encrypted, so share that way only on a network you trust. When two people change the same paragraph, slide or cell at the same moment, the second change is lost, and the same happens when two people edit different shapes on one slide at once. Word comments added or answered while sharing stay on the copy they were made on. If the connection drops, that copy stops following, and you join again to get the current document. Carets show only in a Word document's body, not in its headers, footers or notes, and in a presentation only on the slide you're on.
- **AI agents.** They work in Word documents, presentations and workbooks, not in PDFs, CSV, Markdown, text or pictures. They can only work while Writan is open with the document in a tab. An agent on another computer can't have Writan open a file or download a picture.
- **Recovery and autosave.** Writan reopens your saved tabs when it starts. It also keeps copies of unsaved edits and untitled documents in its own storage, about a second after you stop typing and at least every 10 seconds while you type, and reopens them after a crash or a reload. The last moments before a crash can be missing, and so can an entry you were still typing in a spreadsheet cell or a PDF form field. Clearing the app's data deletes those copies. Autosave and version history work only for files already saved to disk.
- **Very large Office files.** Writan refuses an Office file whose parts come to more than 4 GiB once decompressed. It also refuses one with a single XML part larger than 128 MiB or nested more than 256 elements deep. That second limit is on the XML inside the file, not on pictures and other media. A large document can still use a lot of memory.
- **Saving Office files.** Writan saves the parts of a file it doesn't edit back unchanged. A part you edit is rebuilt, and whatever Writan doesn't model in it gets Writan's defaults. An Excel chart you change, for example, is saved with Excel's default styling.
