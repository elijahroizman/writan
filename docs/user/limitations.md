# What it doesn't do yet

- **Word:** tracked changes are accepted or rejected all at once, not one by one. A comment covers text within one paragraph, and adding or removing comments isn't part of undo. When printing, table borders are left out. Block content controls display their saved content, but you can't edit it or recalculate a table of contents. Office Math equations show as one line of text, the way Word's linear format writes them, rather than laid out as built-up math.
- **PowerPoint:** videos are added as their thumbnail with a link, which is how a link to an online video travels between apps. In Writan's slideshow they play on the slide; in PowerPoint or Keynote, clicking one opens the video in the browser. The slideshow plays slide transitions and object animations a click at a time; Appear, Fade, Fly, Wipe, Split, Zoom and Spin look like PowerPoint's, other entrances and exits fade, other emphasis effects pulse, motion paths don't move, and animations started by clicking a shape don't play. Common 2D charts have read-only previews based on the values saved in the chart. Formatting is approximate. Charts with unsupported types or custom axis scales may still show placeholders. The original parts are kept on save.
- **SmartArt (Word and PowerPoint):** diagrams are drawn from the picture of them Office saves with the file, and can't be edited. A diagram saved without that picture, as some other apps do, shows its text in a plain row of boxes instead of its real layout. Its original parts are kept on save.
- **PDF:** text, checkbox, radio and list form fields can be filled, and a signature field takes a picture of your signature; buttons and form scripts don't do anything, and Writan can't add a certificate (digital) signature. Saving an unchanged PDF preserves its original bytes and digital signatures. Editing a signed PDF can invalidate its signature. Forms made only with Adobe's XFA show their placeholder page. Chinese, Japanese or Korean text in a font the PDF doesn't include (some older PDFs) may not show. In right-to-left PDF paragraphs that also contain space-separated left-to-right words, Writan's built-in PDF viewer/search extractor can reorder adjacent left-to-right words backwards by X coordinate even though the PDF stream encodes visual order cleanly.
- **Text in scanned PDFs and WebP images:** languages other than English download once before Writan can read them. Text read from a scanned PDF is found and selected in Writan but isn't saved into the file, and it's read again each time the PDF opens. Recognition accuracy depends on scan contrast, resolution, and selected language packs; blank source pages with no recognized text are omitted when reconstructing a `.docx`, and Hebrew numbered lists reconstructed into `.docx` use Word's `hebrew2` numbering format, which Writan currently displays with decimal numbers. WebP images open for viewing, OCR and searchable-PDF export; drawing or cropping markup opens a JPEG copy.
- **LaTeX:** needs TeX on the computer. A new, unsaved document can't use other files. Clicking in the PDF doesn't jump to the source (no SyncTeX yet). On Windows, installing and removing TeX are still being tested.
- **Spelling:** Excel workbooks, PDF form fields, LaTeX source, and text in grouped shapes on a slide aren't spell checked. On Windows, Word and PowerPoint use the installed Windows spell checker dictionary for your language (regional variants such as Australian English need that language's Basic typing feature installed in Windows Settings, falling back to another installed English dictionary otherwise). On Linux, Markdown and text files aren't spell checked either, and Word and PowerPoint need a Hunspell dictionary (`hunspell-en-us` or your language's).
- **Excel:** pictures, conditional formatting and data validation show but can't be edited; they're kept as they are when the file is saved. Inserting or deleting rows and columns moves them with their cells. Charts show and can be edited; radar, stock, bubble and surface charts show as a placeholder and are kept as they are unless you change them, and a chart you change is saved with Excel's default styling. A CSV file can't hold charts. About 140 of the common functions are worked out, including SUMIFS, XLOOKUP, IFS and the statistics and date functions; a formula using another one shows `#NAME?` until Excel recalculates it. Formulas and defined names can reference existing Excel tables (for example, `Sales[Amount]`), and inserting or deleting rows updates the table ranges. Writan can't create or rename Excel tables. A CSV file is saved with its values only, so formatting made in Writan is lost when it's closed.

- **Sharing:** it works only while the person sharing has the tab open, and a code lets people join for ten minutes. When Writan can't reach the internet, sharing works only between computers on the same network; then a firewall on the sharing computer has to let Writan accept connections (macOS and Windows ask the first time), and what's sent isn't encrypted, so share that way only on a network you trust. Two people changing the same paragraph, slide or cell at the same moment lose the second change, and two people editing different shapes on one slide at the same moment do too. Word comments added or answered while sharing stay on the copy they were added on. If the connection drops, that copy stops following; join again to get the current document. Carets show only in a Word document's body, not in its headers, footers or notes, and in a presentation only on the slide you're on.

- **Recovery and autosave:** session restore reopens your saved tabs, and crash recovery stores snapshots of unsaved edits and untitled documents in the app's own storage shortly after each edit (roughly a second after stopping, or at least every 10 seconds while typing) so they reopen automatically after a crash or window reload. Edits made in the final moments before a crash or while mid-entry in a spreadsheet cell or PDF form field may not yet be captured, and clearing app data removes recovery snapshots. Autosave and version history apply only to files that have already been saved to a location on disk.

- An Office file with any individual XML part larger than 128 MiB or nested more than 256 elements deep is refused. This limit applies to internal XML parts, not the total file size or ordinary media attachments. Large documents can still use substantial memory.
